## Abstract

LLM research tools present fluent answers supported by citations, yet citations raise trust without lowering the cost of checking them, and only around 10% are ever checked (Ding et al., 2025). Students can therefore paste unsupported claims and fabricated references into work they are accountable for. Explanations reduce over-reliance only when they make verifying cheaper than trusting (Vasconcelos et al., 2023), but no study I reviewed tested whether making claim-level evidence cheap to check increases verification of open-ended LLM answers.

This project is a browser-based writing editor that checks citations at the moment AI-generated text is pasted into a student's work. For each citation it runs a simulated lookup against a local list of eight real papers, compares the claim with one sentence copied from the paper's abstract, and scores the results. Clear problems (a reference not found, matched by title only, or not supported) open a pop-up showing the source sentence, a prompting question and Verify, Edit and Dismiss controls. Likely supported citations get a soft green underline that still counts as unverified. A partial match stays quiet, with a question on click, unless the paste is long or contains several citations, which raises it to a pop-up. This selective design aims to make checking cheap without the cry-wolf effect of flagging every citation.

Testing with 14 cases showed that fabricated, misspelled and unsupported references were flagged as intended. However, matching compares words rather than meaning: only claims worded almost exactly like the stored sentence reached "likely supported", and neither accurate paraphrase tested was recognised as supported. The lookup is simulated, history lasts for the session only, and no user study was run, so the prototype shows the interaction rather than its effect on students' trust. Live lookup, meaning-based matching and saving across reloads are planned.
